# ATLAS Paired Failure-Diagnosis Pilot

**Date:** 2026-07-22

**Models:** Qwen3.6-27B (`qwen35-27b`) vs gpt-5.6-sol/high

**Scope:** one-repetition qualitative comparison on eight held-out FAILED/TIMEOUT traces.

## Integrity result

- Frozen canonical 28-code taxonomy SHA-256: `f9cdff54d6b7f882511368d36686bf7f02b54c44a0bc80c08138baeb59713620`.
- Frozen eval contains 8 unique tasks: 4 FAILED and 4 TIMEOUT; historical source models claude, claude-s4, gpt5, and o4mini each contribute one of each outcome.
- Both arms used the same ordered traces, taxonomy, visible ATLAS system/user prompt, and diagnosis fields.
- Strict post-hoc audit found 8/8 schema- and code-valid raw responses in each arm, zero vendor code coercions, and matching visible prompt hashes for all eight pairs.
- Exact code and category agreement are both 7/8. Qwen selected A.4 four times and B.7 four times; the large model selected A.4 five times and B.7 three times.

## Blinded quality audit

An independent reviewer scored evidence groundedness, root-cause specificity, and recovery actionability from 0–2 per trace and arm. A/B identities were independently randomized per trace and unblinded only after scoring.

| Measure (max 16 each) | Qwen3.6-27B | gpt-5.6-sol/high |
|---|---:|---:|
| Evidence groundedness | **16** | 15 |
| Root-cause specificity | 11 | **14** |
| Recovery actionability | 9 | **16** |
| Total (max 48) | 36 | **45** |
| Pairwise wins | 1 | **7** |

The large-model advantage repeats across both outcome classes:

- FAILED: 23 vs 20; large wins 3/4.
- TIMEOUT: 22 vs 16; large wins 4/4.
- It also repeats across all four historical source-model strata in aggregate, although individual project/source strata remain small or confounded.

## Takeaway

The main difference is not basic trace reading or coarse taxonomy selection. Qwen grounded its evidence at least as well and agreed on the exact code in 7/8 cases. The gap appears after recognition: the large model more often turns the observed failure into a task-specific causal diagnosis and a concrete next repair step. Qwen's TIMEOUT recommendations frequently collapse to “increase budget or switch strategy,” whereas the large model more often identifies the remaining Verus obligation, rlimit bottleneck, unsafe edit, accepted regression, or exact repeated tactic and proposes a targeted recovery.

This supports a repeated qualitative diagnosis-quality difference, especially in recovery actionability. It does not establish diagnosis accuracy, a pure parameter-scale effect, solved-rate improvement, or token-efficiency improvement.

## Caveats and decision

- There are no human failure-code gold labels; the frontier model is a comparison arm, not gold.
- The taxonomy was generated by the large model, which may create vocabulary alignment bias.
- Transports differ: local OpenAI-compatible Qwen vs Codex CLI frontier; only the visible ATLAS prompt is identical.
- The adapter exposes compact traces and explicit final outcomes, not full patches or hidden reasoning.
- One repetition cannot separate model-level behavior from within-model stochasticity.

Decision: retain this result as a mechanism/failure-analysis pilot. Do not rerun taxonomy induction yet. If a stronger paper claim is needed, next run three repetitions per arm on the same frozen eight traces, then compare cross-model disagreement against within-model variability; alternatively add expert gold adjudication.
