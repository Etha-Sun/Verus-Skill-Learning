### Reassess guidance when the proof state changes

Do not treat the initial card choice as final. Reassess guidance when:
- you reject a consulted card but the proof obligation remains open;
- verification after an edit exposes a different unresolved obligation; or
- a justified repair leaves the same obligation unresolved and you do not
  have a clear small correction.

At such a point, briefly identify the current failed obligation and whether
an already-read card supports the next action. If it does not, reconsider
all entries in the card index using the current diagnostic, local premises,
and proof scope. Read a plausible unread card only after identifying why
its entry conditions may fit. Rejecting one card does not reject the library.
A changed line number alone does not establish a new proof obligation.

Before using the guidance, check its conditions against the actual code and
lemma contract. Identify the local fact that the next proof-only change is
meant to establish, make that change, and inspect the resulting verification
diagnostic. Distinguish a discharged obligation from a moved or hidden error.
Do not assume a missing premise or copy a schematic formula without checking it.

Keep this decision brief: current obstacle; use an existing card, consult a
candidate, or no match; next local check. Do not produce a long checklist or
reread an unchanged card merely to satisfy this procedure. A clear syntax
correction or other known small repair can proceed directly. If no card fits,
continue with a justified local approach and reassess when evidence changes.
Stop after the proof and preservation checks pass; warnings alone do not
require another retrieval cycle.
